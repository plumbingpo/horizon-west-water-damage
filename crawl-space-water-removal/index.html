Crawl Space Water Removal: What Horizon West Homeowners Should Know

Water under the house is easy to miss. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

Warning Signs

  • Tide lines or white mineral crust on the piers and foundation walls
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • Corroded metal such as duct straps, pipe hangers and steel posts
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
  • A musty, earthy smell that is strongest near the floor vents
If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Water Pools Below

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Gets Started

Mold can start on damp joists and paper faced insulation. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

What We Do

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Safety Check and Inspection

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Remove Wet Materials

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Final Drying and Checks

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Shifting Piers and Supports

When the ground stays soaked, footings may lose support and piers can tilt or sink. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

Contaminated Water

Water under a house is often not clean. If it came from a drain pipe or from waste laden groundwater it is rated Category 2 or 3, so we suit up and disinfect.

What You Breathe Upstairs

A good share of the air on your first floor rises from the crawl space, so mold spores and damp odors down there end up in the rooms you live in.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Electrical runs, junction boxes and ductwork pass through most crawl spaces. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.
