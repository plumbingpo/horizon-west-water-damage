Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Horizon West Homeowners Should Know

Think of drywall as a wick. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Signs to Look For

  • Dark spots or fuzzy growth at corners and behind furniture
  • Discolored lower drywall with a visible line marking how high water wicked
  • Flooring along the wall that is damp, stained or starting to lift
  • A wall surface that gives, feels mushy or breaks apart under your thumb
  • A wall that feels cool to the touch compared with others
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, wrinkles or peels near the floor

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Map the Moisture

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Wicking Begins

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Days 3 through 7

Drywall Breaks Down

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

After a week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Growth Behind the Paint

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Sections of wall covered by cabinets, a vanity or shelving stay damp because air never reaches them. Checking under the cabinet base and on the reverse face tells us if it is wet.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Once wet, fiberglass insulation stays wet and keeps the studs damp. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Moisture in Exterior Walls

Water in a perimeter wall may reach the sheathing on the far side of the cavity. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.
