What Usually Goes Wrong in Sarasota Homes

Water damage does not always arrive as a sudden gush across the kitchen. Here is what homeowners around Sarasota phone us about most, along with what usually sits behind each one.

Hidden leaks: Small drips from a worn fitting or a tiny hole in a pipe may go unnoticed behind drywall for a long time. Clues include flooring that feels spongy, blistered paint and a higher water bill you cannot explain. Our technicians trace the wet area with moisture meters and thermal cameras, without tearing out more wall than needed. Left alone, constant dampness rots framing and feeds mold, so a minor drip is still worth fixing now.

Sewage: A blocked sewer line sends waste back into the house, usually at the lowest shower, toilet or floor drain. Tree roots, grease buildup and heavy rain overloading the public system are the common triggers. This is Category 3 water, which carries bacteria and should not be handled without protective gear. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. From there it comes in under doors, through window wells and across garage floors. Floodwater from outside picks up soil, fertilizer and whatever else it crossed, so we treat it as contaminated. Homes across Sarasota can see this in any season with a strong enough storm.

Crawlspace: In homes built over a crawl space, few people look underneath, so moisture there builds up unseen. Humidity stays high down there when a pipe drips, the ground cover is damaged or water pools after rain. You may notice cupped floorboards, a stale smell in the living area or insulation hanging loose. We pump out what has collected, dry the joists and subfloor, and show you where the moisture is coming from.

Condensate: An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid day. It is meant to exit through a narrow drain tube, and that tube plugs up with slime and debris. A blockage makes the drip pan spill over, wetting the ceiling or floor near the indoor unit. Most owners learn of it from a ceiling stain or drooping drywall, so have the drain line cleared once a year.
